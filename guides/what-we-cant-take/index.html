What we can't take, and where it goes instead

A short list of things that need a specialist licence, a different vehicle or a different process. We would rather tell you now than turn up and leave them on the drive.

Fridges, freezers and air conditioning units

Why not: they contain refrigerant gas and oil, and the law requires them to be degassed at a licensed facility before anything else happens. A general waste van is not that facility.

Where it goes: your council's household recycling centre takes domestic fridges. Most retailers take the old one away when they deliver a new one, for a fee or free. Businesses need a specialist WEEE collector.

Paint, solvents, chemicals and oils

Why not: liquids and chemicals are hazardous waste. They need a consignment note, a sealed container and a carrier licensed for them.

Where it goes: a tin of paint with a little left in it can be dried out with sand or cat litter and then goes in a normal load as a solid. Full tins, solvents, garden chemicals and engine oil go to the council recycling centre, which takes them from householders. Businesses book a hazardous waste collection.

Tyres

Why not: tyres are banned from landfill, must go to a registered tyre recycler and carry a charge per tyre. We are not set up for them yet.

Where it goes: any garage fitting new tyres takes the old ones. Council recycling centres take a small number from householders. Garages with a stack use a tyre collector.

Car batteries and other batteries

Why not: lead-acid batteries are hazardous and lithium batteries are a fire risk in a mixed load.

Where it goes: a garage or motor factor will take a car battery, and so will the council recycling centre. Small household batteries go in the collection boxes in supermarkets and electrical shops.

Gas bottles and fire extinguishers

Why not: pressurised containers cannot go into a cage with other waste or into a transfer station.

Where it goes: gas bottles belong to the supplier whose name is on them, and they want them back. Fire extinguishers go back to the company that services them, or to the council recycling centre.

Asbestos

Why not: asbestos removal and transport is licensed work with its own packaging, paperwork and disposal sites. Nobody should put it in a general load, including the cement sheets off an old garage roof.

Where it goes: a licensed asbestos contractor. Some council recycling centres take a small amount of cement-bonded asbestos from householders if it is double-bagged, but check with your council first.

Clinical and medical waste

Why not: sharps, dressings and medicines have their own regulated route.

Where it goes: pharmacies take unused medicines. Sharps go back through the GP or the council's clinical waste service.

Things we do take that people think we don't

  • Mattresses and sofas, with a small charge each because they take up space and go to a specialist recycler.
  • Plasterboard, as long as it is kept separate from the rest of the load. It cannot go to landfill mixed with general waste.
  • Washing machines, cookers, dishwashers and tumble dryers, which are scrap steel to us.
  • Rubble, soil and hardcore, by the tonne.
  • Garden waste, sheds and fencing.
  • Scrap metal of every kind, which we pay for. See how Weigh & Pay works.

Questions people ask

I have one fridge in a house clearance. Can you leave it?

Yes. We take everything else and tell you the fridge is staying before we start, so there is no surprise. Many customers book the retailer take-back for it or run it to the recycling centre.

Will you take an empty paint tin?

Yes. Empty and dry, it is a steel tin. Half full, it is a liquid and it stays.

Why does the list change?

Some of these need a licence or equipment we do not have today. If that changes, this page changes with it.
