Skip hire or man and van: which is cheaper?

Most people compare the headline price of a skip with the headline price of a collection and stop there. The real difference is in the permit, the loading and what happens to the space outside your house for a week.

What you pay for with a skip

A skip price covers the skip, the drop-off, the pick-up and the disposal of whatever is in it, up to a level load. Anything above the rim is not supposed to leave with it, and the driver can refuse an overfilled skip or charge for it.

Three costs sit outside the headline price:

  • The permit. If the skip goes on the road rather than your drive, the council has to issue a permit. The skip company usually applies for it, charges you for it and needs a few days' notice. Some roads also need the skip lit and coned.
  • Your labour. You fill it. If the waste is three floors up, that is your afternoon, or your builder's hours on the clock.
  • The things you cannot put in it. Mattresses, fridges, plasterboard in with general waste, tyres and gas bottles all cause trouble at the yard. Most skip firms either refuse them or add a charge per item.

What you pay for with a man and van

A collection like ours is priced by how much space the waste takes on the van, with the loading, the labour and the disposal included. You pick a load size, we agree the price before anything is touched, our driver loads it and the job is finished in one visit. There is no permit, because nothing is left on the road, and nothing sits outside your house.

The price list is on the prices page, from £89 for a mini load to £439 for a full cage. The only extras are for very heavy loads and for long carries that take real time, and both are explained before you book.

Where a man and van wins

  • The waste is ready now and you want it gone today or this week, in one visit.
  • You have no drive, or no room on it, so a skip would need a permit.
  • The waste is upstairs, in a loft, or at the back of a garden, and you do not want to carry it.
  • There is furniture, mattresses, white goods or scrap in the mix. We take those as standard and the scrap comes off the price.
  • You need the paperwork. Every collection comes with a waste transfer note.

Where a skip still wins

We would rather tell you this than have you book the wrong thing.

  • Waste that builds up over weeks. If a building job produces a bit of rubble every day for a month, a skip on the drive is cheaper than several collections. A business account with scheduled collections closes some of that gap, but not all of it.
  • Very heavy inert material in bulk. Several tonnes of hardcore or soil go better in a skip built for it. We take rubble and soil by the tonne, but a cage tipper has a weight limit, and we are honest about it.
  • Sites with no one to agree the load. We like someone to show us the waste. A skip just sits there.

The quick way to decide

If it would fit in a 4, 6 or 8 yard skip and it is ready to go, a collection is almost always cheaper once you count the permit and your own time, and it is done the same day. If the waste is still being made, or it is a mountain of hardcore, hire the skip. If you are not sure, send us a photo and we will tell you straight.

Questions people ask

Do I need a permit for a skip on my own drive?

No. Permits are for skips on the public highway, which includes the road and the pavement outside your house. The rules and the cost are set by each council.

How big is a 6 yard skip compared to your van?

A 6 yard skip holds about 6 cubic yards, level loaded. Our half cage is 7 cubic yards and a full cage is 14, so one van visit clears what a 6 or 8 yard skip would, with the loading included. See load sizes explained.

Can I mix a skip and a collection?

Yes, and plenty of builders do: a skip for the daily rubble, and a collection at the end for the fittings, the furniture and the metal.
